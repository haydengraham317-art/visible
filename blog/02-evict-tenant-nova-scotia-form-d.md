# How to Evict a Tenant for Unpaid Rent in Nova Scotia: The Form D and Form K Process

Rent is late. It has been late before. You are wondering how long you are legally required to
absorb it, and what happens if you act.

Nova Scotia's non-payment process is faster than most landlords expect — and considerably faster
than it used to be. Rules that took effect in April 2025 shortened the timeline significantly. It
is also unforgiving of paperwork errors: serve the wrong form, or serve the right form a day early,
and you start over.

Here is the process, in order, with the timing that actually governs it.

## The short version

| Step | What happens | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rent becomes overdue | Day 1 |
| 2 | Wait out the arrears period | Rent must be **3 days** overdue |
| 3 | Serve **Form D** — Notice to Quit for non-payment | Earliest day 5 |
| 4 | Tenant's window to pay in full | **10 days** from receipt |
| 5 | If unpaid, file **Form K** with Residential Tenancies | After day 10 |
| 6 | Director issues eviction order — **no hearing required** | Often ~1 week |
| 7 | Sheriff enforces if the tenant does not leave | Varies |

Realistically: **three to five weeks** from first missed payment to an enforceable order, when the
tenant does not dispute and your paperwork is clean.

## Step 1 — Wait until the rent is properly overdue

You cannot serve notice the moment rent is late.

Rent must be at least **three days overdue**. The counting is precise, and it is where most
landlords make their first mistake. Rent due on the 1st is counted from the 2nd; the tenant has
through the end of the 3rd day; the earliest you can put Form D in their hands is the **5th**.

Serve on the 4th because you counted from the 1st, and a tenant who disputes it can have the notice
invalidated. Three lost weeks over a single day.

**Before you serve anything, confirm:**

- The rent really is unpaid — check for a pending e-transfer or a partial payment.
- You are counting from the day *after* rent was due.
- You have not accepted a payment that would reset the clock.

## Step 2 — Serve Form D correctly

**Form D** is the Landlord's Notice to Quit for failure to pay rent. Use the current version from
the Residential Tenancies Program; older versions get rejected.

Get these right:

- **The arrears amount must be exact.** Not estimated, not rounded, not including late fees or
  damages — the unpaid *rent*. A wrong number is the single most common reason a notice fails.
- **Name every tenant on the lease.** Anyone who signed must be named.
- **Deliver it properly** and keep proof. If the tenant claims they never received it, you need to
  show otherwise.

Serving Form D does not end the tenancy. It starts a clock.

## Step 3 — The tenant's 10-day window

From the day they receive Form D, the tenant has **10 days** to pay the arrears in full.

**If they pay in full, the tenancy continues.** The notice is spent. You cannot proceed to eviction
on that notice, even if they have been late repeatedly. Take the payment and move on.

**If they pay part of it,** be careful. Accepting partial payment can complicate your position, and
the arrears figure on your notice is no longer accurate. Get advice before accepting anything less
than the full amount.

**If they pay nothing,** you may proceed after day 10.

## Step 4 — File Form K

**Form K** is the streamlined application to the Director of Residential Tenancies to end the
tenancy for unpaid rent.

The important feature: **it does not require a hearing.** Where the tenant has not paid and has not
disputed, the Director can issue an eviction order on the paperwork alone — commonly within about a
week of filing.

This is the part landlords most often do not realise. Nova Scotia's non-payment route is genuinely
quick compared with the multi-month tribunal backlogs in Ontario and BC. What slows it down is
almost never the system — it is landlords waiting months before starting, or filing a defective
Form D.

## Step 5 — Enforcement

An eviction order is not a self-executing event. If the tenant does not leave, enforcement goes
through the **sheriff**. You may not change the locks, remove belongings, or shut off utilities.
"Self-help eviction" is unlawful and turns a case you were winning into one you lose.

## What about evictions that aren't about rent?

Non-payment is the fast track. Everything else is slower and evidence-heavy.

- **Landlord's or family member's own use** — specific notice requirements, and the intention must
  be genuine.
- **Renovations ("renoviction")** — the high bar. Requires an order of the Director, at least
  **three months'** notice, renovations extensive enough to need a **building permit and vacant
  possession**, and **compensation** to the tenant scaled to the size of the building. You cannot
  simply issue notice and renovate.
- **Cause** — since the April 2025 amendments, clearer grounds exist for criminal behaviour,
  disturbing other tenants, repeated late payment, and extraordinary damage. Each demands
  documentation: dates, photographs, written complaints, correspondence.

On repeated late payment specifically: it is a ground, but only if you have *recorded* it. Landlords
who mentally note that rent is "always late" and then try to prove it a year later generally cannot.
Log every late payment in writing, every time.

## Five mistakes that cost landlords months

1. **Serving Form D too early.** Count from the day after rent was due.
2. **Wrong arrears amount.** Rent only. Exact.
3. **Accepting partial payment without advice.** It can undermine the notice you just served.
4. **Waiting too long to start.** Every month of hoping is a month of unrecoverable arrears.
5. **Self-help eviction.** Never change the locks. It converts your strong case into liability.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does it take to evict a tenant in Nova Scotia for unpaid rent?**
Typically three to five weeks from the first missed payment to an enforceable order, assuming the
tenant does not dispute and the paperwork is correct: 3 days of arrears, then Form D, then a 10-day
window, then Form K and roughly a week for the order.

**When can I serve a Form D?**
Once rent is at least three days overdue. For rent due on the 1st, the earliest is generally the 5th.

**What is the difference between Form D and Form K?**
Form D is the notice you serve on the tenant. Form K is the application you file with Residential
Tenancies to obtain the eviction order when the arrears remain unpaid.

**Do I need a hearing to evict for non-payment in Nova Scotia?**
Usually not. The Form K route allows the Director to issue an order without a hearing where the
tenant has not paid and has not disputed.

**What if the tenant pays after I serve the Form D?**
If they pay the full arrears within 10 days, the tenancy continues and the notice is spent.

**Can I change the locks if a tenant stops paying?**
No. Lockouts, removing possessions, and cutting utilities are unlawful. Only the sheriff enforces
an eviction order.

**Can I evict for repeated late payment rather than non-payment?**
It is a recognised ground, but you must be able to document a pattern. Keep a written record of
every late payment as it happens.
