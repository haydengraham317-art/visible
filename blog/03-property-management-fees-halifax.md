# Property Management Fees in Halifax: What Landlords Actually Pay in 2026

Most property management websites will not tell you what they charge. You fill in a form, wait for a
callback, and get a number with no way to judge whether it is reasonable.

We publish ours — **7% to 11% of gross rents**, depending on portfolio size, plus a maintenance
coordination fee. Tenant placement on its own is 75% of the first month's rent.

But a number without context is not much more useful than no number at all. So here is the whole
Halifax picture: what the market charges, what sits inside a headline percentage, the extra fees
that make quotes impossible to compare, and how to work out whether management pays for itself on
your specific unit.

## The market range

In Halifax and across HRM, full-service residential property management generally runs **6% to 11%
of rent**, with most of Atlantic Canada clustering around 7% to 9%.

That is a wide spread, and the spread is not arbitrary. What moves you within it:

- **Unit count.** One condo costs proportionally more to manage than eight units in one building.
  This is the single biggest factor, and it is why our own range starts at 7% and moves down the
  scale as portfolios grow.
- **Property type.** A single-family home with a yard, a driveway and its own furnace takes more
  work than a condo where the building handles the envelope.
- **Geography.** A portfolio spread from Timberlea to Dartmouth costs more to service than four
  units on one street.
- **Service level.** Full management and leasing-only are different products at different prices.
- **Tenancy stability.** Long-tenured, low-maintenance units are cheaper to run, and pricing
  eventually reflects it.

**On the HRM average rent of roughly $2,255 a month, 9% is about $203 per month.**

### Percentage, flat fee, or hybrid

**Percentage of rent** is the most common structure in this market, and it keeps the manager's
revenue tied to your property performing. If rents rise because the unit is well run, the manager
participates; if the unit is neglected and rents slip, so does their fee.

**Flat fee per door** offers predictability and can favour higher-rent units — 9% of $3,000 is $270,
where a flat $180 is less. On a lower-rent unit the maths reverses and the percentage wins.

**Hybrid** models spread through 2026: a smaller base fee plus a reduced percentage, say $100 plus
4%. It splits the difference between predictable revenue for the manager and performance alignment
for the owner.

None of these is inherently better. What matters is that you can see the number before you sign.

## The fees that are not in the headline percentage

This is where quoted prices stop being comparable. A 7% manager with aggressive add-ons can cost
more across a year than a 10% manager whose fee genuinely covers the work.

| Fee | Typical Halifax range | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenant placement / leasing** | 50%–100% of one month's rent | The big one. Charged on each new tenancy |
| **Lease renewal** | $150–$300 | Charged when an existing tenant renews |
| **Setup / onboarding** | $0–$300 | One-time, when you sign on |
| **Maintenance coordination** | Flat fee or % of invoice | Sourcing, scheduling and overseeing vendors |
| **Inspection fees** | $0–$150 each | Some managers include them, some bill separately |
| **Vacancy fee** | Varies | Some charge while a unit sits empty; many do not |
| **Residential Tenancies filings** | Hourly or fixed | Preparing notices and attending hearings |
| **Early termination** | Varies | What it costs to leave the agreement |

**And HST.** Property management is a taxable service in Nova Scotia, so 15% goes on top of the
management fee. A quoted 9% is closer to 10.35% all-in. Quotes almost never include it — ask.

### On maintenance fees specifically

There are two different things here and they are worth separating, because they behave very
differently over a year.

A **percentage markup on invoices** scales with what gets spent. The more the repair costs, the more
the manager earns from it. That structure does not point in the same direction as your interests.

A **flat coordination fee** covers the actual work of maintenance — getting multiple quotes, booking
the vendor, letting them in, checking the job, handling the invoice — and does not grow when the
repair is expensive. The manager's incentive stays where it should be: source competitively, because
they are paid the same either way. That is the structure we use, and it is why we quote it
separately rather than burying it in the percentage.

When you compare managers, ask which of the two you are being offered. It is a more revealing
question than the headline rate.

### Five questions that make quotes comparable

1. What is the **leasing fee**, and does it also apply to renewals?
2. Is maintenance charged as a **flat coordination fee or a percentage of the invoice**?
3. What is billed separately that you would assume is included — inspections, notices, filings?
4. What is the **all-in first-year cost**, including HST, assuming one turnover?
5. What does it cost to **terminate**, and how much notice is required?

Question 4 is the one that exposes real differences. Ask every manager for that single number and
the comparison becomes trivial.

## Is a property manager worth it?

Run it on your own unit rather than in the abstract. Here is a Bedford unit at $2,200 a month,
managed at 9%.

**A year with no turnover** — the normal case when a tenant stays:

- Management: $198 × 12 = $2,376
- HST at 15%: $356
- **Total: about $2,732, or roughly 10.4% of the year's gross rent**

**A year with one turnover:**

- Everything above: $2,732
- Tenant placement at 75% of one month: $1,650
- HST on placement: $248
- **Total: about $4,630, or roughly 17.5% of the year's gross rent**

That second number is the honest one, and most companies would rather you did not calculate it.

It also tells you exactly where the leverage is. **The placement fee is the fee to avoid**, and the
way to avoid it is to not have turnover — which is entirely a function of how carefully the tenant
was screened in the first place and how well they are treated afterwards. That is why we run credit
and background checks, verify employment and income, and actually call previous landlords, rather
than taking the first applicant who fills out the form. A placement that lasts four years costs you
that fee once. A placement that lasts eight months costs it repeatedly, plus the vacancy in between.

**Now the other side of the ledger.** With HRM vacancy near 2.7%, the largest controllable number in
your return is not the management fee — it is **vacancy**. One empty month on that unit is $2,200,
which is close to a full year of management. Cutting an average vacancy from six weeks to three pays
most of the fee by itself.

The rest of the case is risk. A missed four-month rent increase notice costs you twelve months of
the increase. A defective Form D costs weeks of unrecoverable arrears. One bad tenant placement can
cost more than several years of fees. Those are the expensive mistakes, and avoiding them is most of
what you are actually buying.

**Management usually makes sense when:**

- You do not live near the property, or you are out of province
- You own more than one or two units
- Your time is worth more than the fee, hour for hour
- You are not confident with the Residential Tenancies Act and its forms
- You want to be genuinely uninvolved rather than nominally hands-off

**Self-managing is often fine when:**

- You have one unit, close by, with a long-term tenant
- You are comfortable with the legislation and the paperwork
- You can take a maintenance call at 11pm on a Sunday
- The margin is thin enough that the fee changes the investment case

That last point deserves candour: **if a management fee turns your property from profitable to
unprofitable, the problem is usually the property, not the fee.** Worth knowing either way, and a
free rental analysis will tell you which situation you are in before you commit to anything.

## What full service should actually include

At a full-service rate, expect all of this:

- Marketing across the major rental sites, social media and an agent network
- Tenant screening — credit, background, employment and income, rental history, references
- Lease preparation and execution
- Rent collection, arrears follow-up, and legislated notices
- Maintenance coordination with vetted vendors
- Periodic inspections
- Residential Tenancies filings
- Financial statements and year-end reporting for taxes
- Rent reviews and compliant increase notices within the 5% cap
- Owner portal access to statements and maintenance in real time, not a monthly PDF

If a quote sits at the top of the range but several of these are billed separately, it is not really
a full-service rate.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much do property managers charge in Halifax?**
Generally 6% to 11% of rent for full-service residential management, with 7% to 9% typical across
Atlantic Canada. GoldTree charges 7% to 11% of gross rents depending on portfolio size, plus a
maintenance coordination fee.

**How much does property management cost in Bedford, NS?**
Bedford sits within HRM and prices in line with the wider Halifax market — usually 6% to 11% of
rent, depending on property type and unit count.

**Do property management fees include HST?**
Usually not in the quoted rate. Management is an HST-taxable service in Nova Scotia, so expect 15%
on top.

**Are property management fees tax deductible?**
Management fees are generally deductible against rental income. Confirm your situation with your
accountant.

**What is a tenant placement fee?**
A separate charge for advertising, screening and installing a new tenant — commonly 50% to 100% of
one month's rent in this market. GoldTree's is 75%, and it is also available as a standalone service
if you would rather self-manage after the tenant is in place.

**Is a property manager worth it for a single unit?**
It depends on proximity, your time, and your comfort with the legislation. For one nearby unit with
a stable long-term tenant, self-managing is very workable. For out-of-province owners, multiple
units, or anyone who does not want to handle arrears and notices, management usually pays for itself
through lower vacancy and avoided mistakes.

**What is a maintenance coordination fee?**
A charge for sourcing and managing vendors — getting quotes, scheduling, overseeing the work and
handling invoicing. Ask whether it is a flat fee or a percentage of the invoice; a flat fee keeps
the manager's incentive on finding the best price.
