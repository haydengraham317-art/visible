# Property Management Fees in Halifax: What Landlords Actually Pay in 2026

Most property management websites will not tell you what they charge. You fill in a form, wait for
a callback, and get a number with no way to judge whether it is reasonable.

So here are the actual ranges in the Halifax market in 2026, what sits inside the headline
percentage, the extra charges that catch owners out, and how to work out whether management pays
for itself on your specific unit.

## The headline number

In Halifax and across HRM, full-service residential property management generally runs **6% to 10%
of collected rent**, with Atlantic Canada averaging around **7% to 9%**.

Where you land depends on:

- **Unit count.** One condo costs proportionally more to manage than eight units in one building.
- **Property type.** A single-family home with a yard and its own systems takes more work than an
  apartment in a managed building.
- **Location.** Scattered properties across HRM cost more to service than a concentrated portfolio.
- **Service level.** Full management versus leasing-only are different products at different prices.
- **Tenancy stability.** Long-tenured, low-maintenance units are cheaper to manage, and pricing
  reflects it.

**On the HRM average rent of roughly $2,255 a month, 8% is about $180 per month.**

### Percentage, flat fee, or hybrid

**Percentage of collected rent** is the most common model, and the incentives are the right way
round: the manager only earns when rent is actually collected, and gains when the unit is occupied
and rent is at market.

Watch for one distinction. **"Percentage of rent collected" is not the same as "percentage of rent
due."** The first means a vacant or non-paying month costs the manager too. The second means you
pay regardless. Ask which one you are signing.

**Flat fee per door** offers predictability, and can favour higher-rent units — 8% of $3,000 is
$240, where a flat $180 is less. On a lower-rent unit the maths reverses.

**Hybrid** models have spread in 2026: a smaller base fee plus a reduced percentage — for example a
$100 base plus 4%. It splits the difference between predictable revenue for the manager and
performance alignment for the owner.

## The fees that are not in the headline percentage

This is where quoted prices stop being comparable. A 7% manager with aggressive add-ons can cost
more over a year than a 10% manager whose fee genuinely covers everything.

| Fee | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Tenant placement / leasing** | 50%–100% of one month's rent | The big one. Charged on every new tenancy |
| **Lease renewal** | $150–$300 | Charged when an existing tenant renews |
| **Setup / onboarding** | $0–$300 | One-time, when you sign on |
| **Maintenance markup** | 0%–15% of invoice | A percentage added to contractor invoices |
| **Inspection fees** | $0–$150 each | Some include periodic inspections, some bill them |
| **Vacancy fee** | Varies | Some charge during vacancy; many do not |
| **Eviction / hearing attendance** | Hourly or fixed | Filing and representation at Residential Tenancies |
| **Early termination** | Varies | What it costs to leave the agreement |

**And HST.** Property management services are taxable in Nova Scotia. HST is charged on top of the
management fee, so a quoted 8% is closer to 9.2% all-in. Ask whether quoted numbers include it —
they usually do not.

### Five questions that make quotes comparable

1. Is the fee on rent **collected** or rent **due**?
2. What is the **leasing fee**, and does it apply to renewals?
3. Is there a **markup on maintenance invoices**?
4. What is the **all-in first-year cost**, including HST, assuming one turnover?
5. What does it cost to **terminate**, and how much notice is required?

Question 4 is the one that exposes real differences. Ask every manager for that single number.

## Is a property manager worth it?

Run it on your own unit rather than in the abstract.

**A worked example.** A Bedford unit renting at $2,200, managed at 8% plus HST, with one turnover in
the year and a leasing fee of half a month's rent:

- Management: $176 × 12 = $2,112
- Leasing fee: $1,100
- HST at 15%: ~$482
- **Total: roughly $3,694, or about 14% of the year's gross rent**

That is the honest cost, and it is meaningfully more than the headline 8% implies.

**Now the other side.** In a market where HRM vacancy sits near 2.7%, the largest controllable
number in your return is not the management fee — it is **vacancy**. One vacant month on that unit
is $2,200, which is more than half the annual management cost. Cutting an average vacancy from six
weeks to three pays for a good chunk of the fee by itself.

The rest of the case is risk. A missed four-month rent increase notice costs you twelve months of
the increase. A defective Form D costs weeks of arrears. One bad tenant placement can cost far more
than a year of fees. These are the expensive mistakes, and they are exactly what a manager who does
this daily is there to prevent.

**Management usually makes sense when:**

- You do not live near the property, or you are out of province
- You own more than one or two units
- Your time is worth more than the fee, hour for hour
- You are not confident with the Residential Tenancies Act and its forms
- You want to be genuinely uninvolved rather than nominally hands-off

**Self-managing is often fine when:**

- You have one unit, close by, with a long-term tenant
- You are comfortable with the legislation and the paperwork
- You can take a maintenance call at 11pm on a Sunday
- The margin is thin enough that the fee changes the investment case

That last point deserves candour: **if a management fee turns your property from profitable to
unprofitable, the problem is usually the property, not the fee.** Worth knowing either way.

## What should be included at a full-service rate

At the upper end of the range, expect all of this:

- Marketing, listing, and showings
- Tenant screening — credit, employment, references, previous landlords
- Lease preparation on the standard form and proper execution
- Rent collection, arrears follow-up, and legislated notices
- Maintenance coordination and a 24/7 emergency line
- Periodic inspections with written reports
- Residential Tenancies filings and attendance
- Monthly statements and year-end reporting for taxes
- Rent reviews and compliant increase notices within the 5% cap

If a quote is at the top of the range but several of these are billed separately, it is not really a
full-service rate.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much do property managers charge in Halifax?**
Generally 6% to 10% of collected rent for full-service residential management, with 7% to 9% typical
across Atlantic Canada. Flat-fee and hybrid structures are also available.

**How much does property management cost in Bedford, NS?**
Bedford sits within HRM and prices in line with the wider Halifax market — usually 6% to 10% of
collected rent, depending on property type and unit count.

**Do property management fees include HST?**
Usually not in the quoted rate. Management services are HST-taxable in Nova Scotia, so expect 15%
on top of the fee.

**Are property management fees tax deductible?**
Management fees are generally a deductible expense against rental income. Confirm treatment for
your situation with your accountant.

**What is a tenant placement fee?**
A separate charge for finding and installing a new tenant, commonly 50% to 100% of one month's rent.
It is the largest add-on in most agreements — always ask about it.

**Is a property manager worth it for a single unit?**
It depends on proximity, your time, and your comfort with the legislation. For one nearby unit with
a stable long-term tenant, self-managing is very workable. For out-of-town owners, multiple units,
or anyone who does not want to handle arrears and notices, management usually pays for itself
through lower vacancy and avoided mistakes.

**What is the difference between rent collected and rent due?**
Fees on rent *collected* mean the manager is only paid when money actually arrives — so vacancy and
arrears cost them too. Fees on rent *due* are charged regardless. The first aligns better with your
interests.
