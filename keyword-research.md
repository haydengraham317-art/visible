## 0. Read this first — how the volume numbers were produced

I do **not** have API access to Ahrefs, Semrush, or Google Keyword Planner in this environment, and Google's
autocomplete endpoint is blocked by the network policy here. Every `Est. MSV` figure below is a **modelled
estimate**, not a number pulled from a keyword tool.

The model: Halifax CMA population ≈ 500k (~1.2% of Canada). National-level query volumes were scaled to
Nova Scotia / HRM share, then adjusted for the share of the population who are rental **owners** vs **tenants**
(roughly 1:12), and for observed SERP competition depth.

**Treat these as relative priority rankings, not absolute traffic forecasts.** Before committing budget,
re-run the seed list through Ahrefs or Keyword Planner set to Canada / Nova Scotia. The *clustering,
intent classification, and priority order* below hold regardless of the exact volumes.

## 1. The strategic problem

GoldTree sells to **rental owners**, but the overwhelming majority of NS rental search volume comes from
**tenants**. Chasing raw volume gets traffic that will never convert.

The high-value overlap is **landlord compliance anxiety**: queries where the searcher is an owner, is
worried about getting something legally wrong, and is one bad experience away from outsourcing the whole
job. Nova Scotia is unusually rich in these queries because the province has:

- A **5% rent cap** running to **31 Dec 2027** (extended by Bill 467)
- A **4-month** written notice requirement for rent increases (longest in Canada)
- An open, politically contested **fixed-term lease loophole** the province has said it will not close
- A **2.7% vacancy rate** and 5.4% YoY asking-rent growth — so owners are actively repricing

That combination means NS landlords search constantly, and they search with money on the line.

**Strategy: win landlord-compliance search, convert on operational pain.**

## 5. Technical & on-page notes

- **Every post needs `LocalBusiness` + `FAQPage` schema.** FAQ schema is what earns the PAA box and
  AI Overview citation. Each drafted post includes its FAQ block ready to mark up.
- **Author byline + credentials.** Google's helpful-content systems weight E-E-A-T heavily on
  legal-adjacent (YMYL) topics. Publish under a named person at GoldTree, not "Admin."
- **Link out to `novascotia.ca`.** Citing the primary legal source is a trust signal on YMYL content,
  and it is the honest thing to do — forms and dates change.
- **Internal linking:** every post links to the Free Rental Analysis CTA and to at least two sibling
  posts. Service pages should link *down* to blogs; blogs link *up* to service pages.
- **Update cadence:** Cluster B and C posts must be re-dated and re-checked at minimum every January,
  and immediately on any legislative change. Stale legal content ranks badly and creates real liability.
- **Legal disclaimer on every tenancy-law post.** Included in the drafts.

## 6. Honest caveats

- Volumes are modelled (see §0). Validate in a real keyword tool before allocating budget.
- Nova Scotia tenancy law is actively changing — the rent cap was extended to 2027 by Bill 467, the
  standard-form-lease regulations were amended effective 6 May 2026, and eviction timelines have been
  revised. **Every legal fact in the drafts was verified against sources current as of August 2026 and
  must be re-verified before publishing.**
- Form letters in particular are a known trap: sources disagree on which form covers rent arrears. The
  drafts follow the Government of Nova Scotia's own published guide (`Form D — Landlord's Notice to Quit
  for Rental Arrears`). Confirm against the official residential tenancy forms page before you publish.
