# How Much Rent Can You Charge in Halifax? 2026 Rents by Area

It is the first question every rental owner in HRM asks, and the hardest one to get a straight answer to —
because the published numbers disagree with each other by hundreds of dollars a month.

One source says the average Halifax two-bedroom rents for about $1,650. Another says $2,550. Both are
correct. They are measuring completely different things, and understanding the difference is the single
most useful thing you can learn about pricing a rental in this market.

---

## Halifax rents in 2026: the numbers

**Asking rents** — what units currently listed are advertised at, as of mid-2026:

| Area | 1 bedroom | 2 bedroom | 3 bedroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Halifax | ~$2,110 | ~$2,550 | — |
| Dartmouth | ~$1,740 | ~$2,275 | ~$2,575 |
| Bedford (Bedford West) | ~$1,650–$1,800 | — | — |

Peninsula Halifax commands the highest rents in the region. Dartmouth, Bedford and Sackville are
consistently more affordable, though the gap has narrowed as demand pushed outward from the peninsula.

**Market conditions:**

- **Vacancy rate: 2.7%** — improved from 2.1% as record rental construction began delivering, but still
  tight, and still below most major Canadian markets including Vancouver and Toronto
- **Asking rent growth: +5.4% year over year** for a two-bedroom. For comparison, Toronto ran **-1.1%**
  over the same period — Halifax is one of the few Canadian markets where rents are still climbing
  meaningfully
- **CMHC sample two-bedroom rents grew 6.7%** in 2025

## Why the published averages differ so wildly

This trips up almost every owner, so it is worth being precise.

**CMHC average rent** measures what tenants across the whole existing stock are actually paying, including
people who have lived in the same unit for eight years. Those tenancies are held down by Nova Scotia's 5%
rent cap. The purpose-built two-bedroom average of roughly **$1,650** is a portrait of the *installed
base*.

**Asking rent** measures what is being advertised on the open market today, where the cap does not apply
because the tenant is new. That is the **~$2,550** figure.

The spread between those two numbers — call it $900 a month on a two-bedroom — is the accumulated effect
of six years of capped increases on long-standing tenancies.

**Two practical consequences for owners:**

1. **If you have a long-term tenant, you are probably well below market.** Not because you priced badly,
   but because the cap only permits 5% a year while asking rents rose faster. This is worth knowing and
   planning around — not panicking about.

2. **Do not price a vacant unit off the CMHC average.** It describes tenancies signed years ago. Price
   off current comparable listings, or you will leave several hundred dollars a month on the table for
   the entire length of the next tenancy.

## How to price your own unit

Market averages are a starting point, not an answer. A two-bedroom in a walk-up in Fairview and a
two-bedroom in a new build near the waterfront are not the same product.

**Pull real comparables.** Five to ten currently-listed units matching yours on bedrooms, bathrooms,
square footage, parking, and — critically — neighbourhood. Listings, not sold data. What matters is what
comparable units are asking *right now*.

**Adjust for what you actually include.** In Halifax, heat and hot water included is a substantial premium,
particularly for anything listed between October and March. In-suite laundry, parking, and a dishwasher
each move the number. Utilities-included units advertise higher and net differently — know which you are
comparing against.

**Adjust for condition honestly.** Owners systematically overrate their own properties. Original kitchen,
older windows, dated bathroom — that is not a top-of-market unit no matter how well maintained it is.

**Then check your assumption against vacancy cost.** This is the step most owners skip, and it is the one
that costs the most.

### The overpricing arithmetic

Say comparables suggest $2,400 and you list at $2,550 to see what happens.

- Overpricing by $150 typically adds **three to six weeks** of vacancy in a 2.7%-vacancy market.
- One month empty on a $2,400 unit = **$2,400 gone**, permanently.
- Recovering that $2,400 at $150/month extra takes **16 months**.
- And you have to actually find a tenant willing to pay the premium — most units listed above market
  eventually reduce, having already banked the vacancy.

Meanwhile the correctly priced unit was earning from day one, and typically drew a deeper applicant pool,
which means better screening leverage and a better-quality tenancy.

**A well-priced unit that rents in ten days beats an ambitiously priced one that rents in fifty.** Almost
always. Run this calculation before you list.

## Is Halifax still a good rental market in 2026?

For owners, the fundamentals remain genuinely strong. Vacancy is tight at 2.7%, asking rents are still
growing at 5.4% while several major Canadian markets have gone flat or negative, and population growth
continues to outpace completions despite record construction.

The two things to plan around:

**The rent cap compresses in-place yield.** Five percent annually to the end of 2027 means a long-held
tenancy will drift below market. That is not a reason to churn tenants — turnover costs typically run one
to two months' rent once you count vacancy, cleaning, painting, advertising and screening — but it does
mean pricing correctly *at the start* of a tenancy matters far more here than in an uncapped market. The
number you set on day one is the number you compound from.

**Operating costs are rising faster than 5%.** Property taxes, insurance, and maintenance in HRM are not
respecting the cap. Owners running thin margins on older stock are getting squeezed from both ends.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the average rent in Halifax in 2026?**
Asking rents for a one-bedroom in Halifax average around $2,110 and two-bedrooms around $2,550 as of
mid-2026. CMHC's average for purpose-built two-bedrooms across the existing stock is considerably lower —
around $1,650 — because it includes long-standing tenancies held down by the rent cap.

**What is the average rent in Dartmouth?**
Approximately $1,740 for a one-bedroom, $2,275 for a two-bedroom and $2,575 for a three-bedroom as of
mid-2026.

**What is the average rent in Bedford?**
Suburban one-bedroom rents in Bedford West average roughly $1,650 to $1,800 in 2026.

**What is the vacancy rate in Halifax?**
2.7%, up from 2.1% as record levels of rental construction began delivering. Still tight by national
standards.

**Are rents still going up in Halifax?**
Yes. Average asking rent for a two-bedroom rose 5.4% year over year into 2026, while Toronto fell 1.1%
over the same period.

**How do I know if my rent is below market?**
Compare against current listings for similar units in your neighbourhood, not against CMHC averages. If
you have had the same tenant for several years under the 5% cap, you are very likely below market.

**How much rent can I charge for a new tenant?**
The 5% rent cap applies to existing tenants only. For a genuinely new tenancy you may set rent at market.
