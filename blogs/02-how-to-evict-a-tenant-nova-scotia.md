# How to Evict a Tenant in Nova Scotia (2026): Forms, Timelines and Real Costs

Nobody buys a rental property planning to evict someone. But rent stops arriving, or the damage gets
serious, or the complaints from the neighbours stop being occasional — and suddenly you need to know
exactly how this works in Nova Scotia.

The honest summary: it is slower than most owners expect, the paperwork is unforgiving, and the single
most expensive mistake is trying to shortcut it.

Here is the actual process.

---

## Before anything else: what you cannot do

Nova Scotia, like every province, prohibits **self-help eviction**. Regardless of how much rent is owed
or how badly the tenant has behaved, you may not:

- Change the locks
- Shut off heat, power, or water
- Remove the tenant's belongings
- Remove doors or windows
- Threaten or intimidate the tenant into leaving

Doing any of these exposes you to an order to let the tenant back in, compensation payable to the tenant,
and a Residential Tenancies file that will follow you. It reliably converts a recoverable rent-arrears
problem into a much more expensive one.

A tenancy in Nova Scotia ends one of two ways: the tenant agrees to leave, or the Director of Residential
Tenancies orders it. There is no third route.

---

## Step 1: Confirm you have a legal ground

You cannot evict simply because you would prefer a different tenant. Nova Scotia's Residential Tenancies
Act sets out the permitted grounds, and the common ones are:

- **Non-payment of rent** — by a wide margin the most common
- **Breach of a statutory or lease obligation** — for example subletting without permission, or serious
  damage beyond normal wear
- **Interfering with other tenants' reasonable enjoyment** of the property
- **Landlord's own use**, or use by a close family member
- **Demolition, or major repairs and renovations** — governed by specific sections of the Act with their
  own notice and compensation requirements
- **Foreclosure** by a lender

Note what is not on that list: wanting to raise the rent above the 5% cap. Using an eviction ground as
cover for a rent increase is an abuse of process and tenants dispute it successfully.

---

## Step 2: Serve the right Notice to Quit

### For unpaid rent — Form D

The Government of Nova Scotia publishes **Form D — Landlord's Notice to Quit for Rental Arrears** for
this ground. Two timing rules govern it:

1. **Rent must be at least 15 days overdue** before you can serve Form D.
2. **The quit date must be no sooner than 10 days after the form is successfully served.** Counting starts
   the day *after* service — served on the 5th, you begin counting on the 6th.

So from a missed rent payment, the fastest realistic path to a quit date is roughly **25 days**, and only
if you serve promptly and correctly on day 15.

### For other grounds — Form F

Notices to Quit for other circumstances use a different form with different notice periods depending on
the ground and the type of tenancy. Own-use and renovation evictions in particular carry longer notice
requirements and, in some cases, compensation obligations.

> **Check the form before you serve it.** Nova Scotia has revised its tenancy forms and regulations
> repeatedly — the standard form of lease regulations were amended again effective 6 May 2026. Third-party
> websites are frequently out of date on which form covers which ground. Pull the current version from the
> [official residential tenancy forms page](https://novascotia.ca/residential-tenancy-forms) every single
> time. Serving a superseded form restarts your clock.

### Serving it properly

Service is where otherwise sound evictions collapse. You need to be able to prove the tenant received the
notice, on a specific date. Personal delivery with a witness, or a method that generates a receipt, is
worth the extra effort — because if the tenant disputes and you cannot establish the service date, your
10-day count has no starting point and the notice fails.

---

## Step 3: What happens if the tenant disputes

The tenant has **10 days** from receiving the notice to file a written dispute — an Application to
Director — with the Residential Tenancies Program.

If they do not dispute and they do not leave, you apply to the Director for an order.

If they do dispute, the matter goes to a hearing before a Residential Tenancy Officer. Come prepared:

- The lease
- A complete rent ledger showing every payment and every arrear
- Your Form D and proof of service, with dates
- All written communication with the tenant
- Photographs, inspection reports, or third-party complaints where damage or disturbance is the ground

Officers decide on evidence, not on how reasonable you sound. Owners who arrive with a tidy paper trail
generally win; owners relying on recollection generally do not. You are not required to have a lawyer,
and for a straightforward arrears case most owners do not use one.

Either party may appeal the decision to Small Claims Court within the statutory window.

---

## Step 4: Enforcement

An order in your favour does not physically remove anyone. If the tenant still will not leave, enforcement
runs through the courts and the sheriff. This adds time, and it adds cost.

---

## How long does an eviction actually take in Nova Scotia?

There is no fixed answer, but a realistic range for a straightforward, undisputed arrears case is roughly
**six to ten weeks** from the first missed rent payment to vacant possession:

| Stage | Typical elapsed time |
|---|---|
| Rent missed → eligible to serve Form D | 15 days |
| Service → earliest quit date | 10+ days |
| Dispute window | 10 days |
| Application to Director → hearing | Several weeks |
| Order → enforcement, if needed | Additional weeks |

**A disputed eviction commonly runs three to four months or longer.** Plan your cash flow accordingly,
and assume the arrears keep accruing throughout — because they generally do, and recovering them from a
tenant who could not pay in the first place is often the harder problem.

## What an eviction really costs

The filing fee is the smallest line on the page. The real cost of a single eviction on a $1,800/month
Halifax unit typically looks more like:

- **Lost rent through the process:** $3,600–$7,200
- **Arrears you never recover:** frequently the majority of what is owed
- **Turnover:** cleaning, paint, repairs — $1,000–$4,000, more where there is real damage
- **Vacancy while re-listing and screening:** 2–6 weeks
- **Your time:** the item owners consistently underestimate

Call it **$6,000 to $12,000** in a normal case. Which is the argument for the boring, unglamorous work
that prevents it.

## The cheaper strategy: not getting here

Almost every eviction we see traces back to one of two things — a tenant who should not have been placed,
or arrears that were allowed to drift.

**Screen properly.** Credit and background checks, employment and income verification, a full rental
history review, and actual reference calls to previous landlords. Not the first applicant who seems
pleasant and can pay a deposit. In Nova Scotia you cannot charge an applicant for a credit check, and you
cannot discriminate on protected grounds including source of income — but you are entitled to verify
that an applicant can afford the unit, and you should.

**Act on arrears immediately.** A tenant three days late is a conversation. A tenant sixty days late is a
hearing. The owners who get badly hurt are almost always the ones who let it slide for a couple of months
hoping it would resolve itself. Consistent, documented follow-up from day one resolves most arrears
without any process at all — and where it does not, you have the ledger you will need.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I evict a tenant in Nova Scotia?**
Confirm you have a legal ground, serve the correct Notice to Quit (Form D for rental arrears), wait out
the notice and dispute periods, then apply to the Director of Residential Tenancies for an order if the
tenant does not leave. You cannot evict a tenant yourself.

**How much rent must be overdue before I can evict in Nova Scotia?**
Rent must be at least 15 days overdue before you can serve Form D for rental arrears.

**What is Form D in Nova Scotia?**
Form D is the Landlord's Notice to Quit for Rental Arrears — the notice served on a tenant who has failed
to pay rent. The quit date must be no sooner than 10 days after the form is successfully served.

**How long does it take to evict a tenant in Nova Scotia?**
Roughly six to ten weeks for a straightforward undisputed arrears case. A disputed eviction commonly runs
three to four months or longer.

**Can I change the locks if my tenant stops paying rent?**
No. Self-help eviction is prohibited in Nova Scotia. Changing locks, removing belongings or cutting
utilities can result in an order to readmit the tenant plus compensation payable to them.

**Can I evict a tenant so I can move in myself?**
Landlord's own use is a recognised ground, but it has its own notice requirements and is scrutinised
closely where a tenant disputes it. Verify current requirements before serving notice.

**Do I need a lawyer to evict a tenant in Nova Scotia?**
Not required. Most straightforward arrears cases are handled by the landlord or their property manager.
Complex cases — renovation evictions, large damage claims, appeals — are worth legal advice.

**What happens if the tenant disputes the notice?**
They have 10 days from receiving the notice to file an Application to Director. The matter is then heard
by a Residential Tenancy Officer, who decides based on the evidence both sides present.
